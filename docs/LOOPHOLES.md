# Loopholes — the sixteen ways this fails, and where the answer lives

Every row names the URL to open if challenged. If a response is a declared stub, it says so; that is
the point of the table.

| # | The failure | Milan's response | Where it is implemented | Open this |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A challenge is routed and then quietly forgotten.** | Every non-terminal state opens a durable deadline row in the same transaction as the state change, and a CI query fails the build if any challenge anywhere has none. | `lib/sla/deadlines.ts` (all 22 states), `lib/db/stateMachine.ts`, `tests/invariant.test.ts` | `/gov/sla` |
| 2 | **Nobody claims it, and it dies on a list.** | Three ladders. WIDEN at +7d to five more institutions, OPEN_ALL at +14d to every HEI in the state, BREACH at +21d onto a public bounty board, GRAND_CHALLENGE at +45d. | `lib/sla/actions.ts`, `lib/sla/reaper.ts` | `/bounties`, then `/demo` → +21 days |
| 3 | **A team claims it to block others, then does nothing.** | PROPOSAL_DUE nudges the lead and HOD at +14d and releases the claim at +21d, returning the challenge to ROUTED. The prior team's work is preserved and still credited. | `proposalDue()` in `lib/sla/actions.ts` | `/gov/sla` — "released undelivered" |
| 4 | **A team goes silent mid-project.** | SILENT_30 flags it publicly AT_RISK and nudges the mentor; SILENT_45 opens fork rights, and a fork credits the first team in `credit_edges`. Both are measured from `last_activity_at`, not from the claim. | `silent30()`, `silent45()` | `/c/<id>` — the AT_RISK flag |
| 5 | **The university quietly drops the citizen from the acknowledgements.** | The acknowledgement is not in the paper. It is a credit edge on an append-only chain, rendered publicly, with a citation string that puts the citizen in the author position before the institution. | `components/credit-chain.tsx`, `lib/credit/citation.ts` | `/c/JH-2026-GUM-0001#credit`, `/credit/<userId>` |
| 6 | **Two people report the same problem and one is discarded.** | Duplicates are signal. S3 merges and increments corroborations; the OLDER report always survives, so the first person to notice stays the originator whatever order a batch runs in. Both reporters are credited on MERGED. | `lib/ai/stages/s3.ts` | `/c/<id>` — corroborator nodes |
| 7 | **Brigading — one person files fifty reports to inflate a score.** | One account, one corroboration per challenge, by a unique index; corroboration weight decays with distance from the incident; a 5-per-hour submission rate limit. **Declared partial:** the decaying trust score has columns and no writer. | `corroborations_challenge_user_uniq`, `lib/db/rateLimit.ts` | `/c/<id>#priority` |
| 8 | **The AI misclassifies and nobody notices.** | Every model call writes an `ai_runs` row with its provider, fallback level, confidence and latency. Low confidence goes to a human queue rather than being written. Every correction becomes labelled training data. | `lib/ai/providers/chain.ts`, `training_corrections` | `/admin/ai-runs`, `/admin/triage` |
| 9 | **The AI takes a consequential action alone.** | Severity ≥ 0.70 writes the shortlist with `notified_at = null` and sends nothing until a District Collector confirms. Every override carries a mandatory written reason. | `persistRoutes()`, `app/(gov)/gov/gate/` | `/gov/gate` |
| 10 | **The routing reason is a hallucination.** | The model is handed the top three contributing terms and nothing else, and `guardReason()` rejects any sentence containing a number that was not in those facts. A rejected sentence falls back to a template over the same terms. | `guardReason()` in `lib/ai/routing.ts`, 15 guardrail tests | `/c/<id>` — the shortlist reasons |
| 11 | **A firm patents the student's work.** | Publication writes a ledger entry committing to the file's own SHA-256 at a timestamp. That does not stop a patent being filed — nothing can — it makes the work prior art, which is what stops one being granted over it. The prior-art panel says exactly that. | `lib/artifacts/publish.ts`, the prior-art panel | `/artifacts/<id>` |
| 12 | **"Restricted" is used to make a citizen's problem disappear.** | Title, problem and abstract are public regardless of licence, and the publish form says so before the choice is made. A restricted licence restricts the file, never the knowledge that the work exists. | `lib/artifacts/publish.ts`, `PublishForm` | `/artifacts/<id>` on a RESTRICTED artifact |
| 13 | **A restricted file is read anonymously.** | There is no anonymous read. A verified identity requests access with a stated purpose, the lead grants or denies, and every download writes an `access_log` row and an ACCESS ledger entry visible to the team and to the originating citizen. | `mayDownload()`, `app/api/artifacts/download` | `/artifacts/<id>` — "Who has read this" |
| 14 | **An implementer claims a fix that never happened.** | IMPLEMENTED is a claim and is not terminal. The counter moves only when the person who reported the problem answers. "No, nothing changed" marks the claim disputed, returns the challenge off the happy path and tells the DC. | `app/(citizen)/me/verify/[token]/actions.ts` | `/me/verify/<token>`, `/stats` |
| 15 | **The impact number is inflated by counting publications and funding.** | One definition of the counter exists in the codebase and every surface reads it. Unconfirmed claims render grey with "claimed, not confirmed" everywhere, including the CSR export, and the confirmation gap is shown as a number and a chart rather than hidden. | `lib/impact/counter.ts`, `components/impact-counter.tsx` | `/stats`, `/gov`, `/industry/csr` |
| 16 | **An individual with no legal entity is locked out.** | An individual may claim as an independent innovator with personal credit only; their employer is never named. The UI states that a legal entity is needed to receive money, not to participate. **Declared partial:** the role is in the enum with no dedicated onboarding screen. | `roleEnum` INDEPENDENT_INNOVATOR, `/industry/discover` footer | `/industry/discover` |

## Two more a judge usually asks

| The question | The answer | Open this |
|---|---|---|
| "Why not just use CPGRAMS?" | CPGRAMS routes complaints with a known fix to an accountable officer. Milan routes unsolved problems to a lab, with a clock. When S1 detects a grievance it forwards it and shows the citizen the exact JSON payload it would POST. | `/c/<id>` on a forwarded grievance |
| "Why not a blockchain?" | A SHA-256 hash chain plus a public timestamp gives the same non-repudiation at zero cost and zero latency, and you can verify it in your own browser right now. | `/ledger` → Verify chain |
